# Canonical policy evidence from Front and OME

Per the current review rule, outbound Front messages authored by Kushal/Vorto/
Bainbridge and recruiter statements in OME calls are canonical evidence. The
following is a normalized evidence map; it is not a replacement for source
documents and dynamic program terms must be revalidated before answering.

## Usable policy evidence

- **Onboarding:** registration link, carrier packet, signed documents, insurance,
  background check/Evident, RMIS, and an onboarding call are recurring steps.
- **Insurance/compliance:** insurance must be on file with the designated MCP
  system; a COI emailed directly to recruiting may not be sufficient. Front
  messages repeatedly cite 1M general liability, 1M auto, and 100k cargo, plus
  region-specific certificates such as CARB/Clean Truck.
- **RMIS/Evident timing:** Front messages cite RMIS completion within 30 days and
  Evident completion within 14 days in the applicable onboarding flow.
- **Authority:** multiple Kushal messages state a minimum of 6 months continuous
  authority (and sometimes request proof of 180 days).
- **Training:** OME recruiter statements say formal training is not required in
  some flows, while Front messages provide mandatory web/driver training in
  others. The answer must therefore depend on the carrier/program context.
- **Factoring/payment:** OME commonly describes next-business-day pay with no
  factoring fees, while a Front message explicitly allows factoring for the first
  three weeks with a notice of assignment. These are context- or program-specific
  and cannot be collapsed into one universal rule.
- **Bonuses:** amounts, locations, shift requirements, and deadlines vary by
  program/week. Front and OME provide canonical examples, but the agent must not
  present an old amount as current without a current source.

## Required handling

Authority eligibility and training currently contain conflicting canonical
evidence. Until a current program policy resolves the conflict, the KB should
surface the relevant source context and escalate rather than produce a single
unqualified answer.
